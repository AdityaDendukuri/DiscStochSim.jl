262-\section{Methodology: Flux-Based Adaptive FSP}
--
264-
265-This section introduces a state space adaptation approach that preserves network connectivity through flux-aware truncation. We first define and characterize probability flux as a connectivity measure, argue that naive probability-based pruning can destroy essential pathways, then construct an algorithm that maintains structural integrity of the state-space graph while controlling computational cost.

267-\begin{definition}[Outgoing probability flux]
268-\label{def:flux}
269:Let $w(\boldsymbol{x}) := \sum_{k=1}^M \alpha_k(\boldsymbol{x}) = -\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}}$ denote the total exit rate from state $\boldsymbol{x}$. The \textit{outgoing probability flux} from state $\boldsymbol{x}$ at time $t$ is
270-\begin{equation}
271-\Phi(\boldsymbol{x},t) := p(\boldsymbol{x},t) \cdot w(\boldsymbol{x}).
272-\end{equation}
273-\end{definition}
274-
275-[Omitted long context line]
276-
277-\begin{definition}[Total system flux]
278:The total probability flux of the system at time $t$ over state set $J$ is
279-\begin{equation}
280:\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(J,t) := \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in J} \Phi(\boldsymbol{x},t) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in J} p(\boldsymbol{x},t) \cdot w(\boldsymbol{x}).
281-\end{equation}
282-\end{definition}
283-
284-\begin{proposition}[Total flux properties]
285:\label{prop:total-flux}
286:For the full state space $\mathcal{X}$ and any time $t \geq 0$, the total system flux satisfies
287-\begin{equation}
288:\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(\mathcal{X},t) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{X}} p(\boldsymbol{x},t) \cdot w(\boldsymbol{x}) = -\boldsymbol{p}(t)^\top \mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{A}).
289-\end{equation}
290:Since $\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}} p(\boldsymbol{x},t) = 1$ for all $t$, the total flux represents a weighted average of exit rates across the state space. For reaction networks with mass-action or similar kinetics where exit rates grow at most polynomially with state values, $\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(\mathcal{X},t)$ remains bounded on compact time intervals and tracks the instantaneous activity level of the system.
291-\end{proposition}
292-
293-\begin{proof}
294-By definition of the generator matrix,
295-\begin{align}
296:\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(\mathcal{X},t) &= \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{X}} p(\boldsymbol{x},t) \cdot (-\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}}) = -\boldsymbol{p}(t)^\top \mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{A}).
297-\end{align}
298:Since the diagonal entries $\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}} = -\sum_{k=1}^M \alpha_k(\boldsymbol{x})$ depend only on the reaction network structure and state $\boldsymbol{x}$, the total flux evolves with the probability distribution. 
299-\end{proof}

372-\begin{remark}[Total flux versus boundary outflux]
373:\label{rem:total-vs-boundary-flux}
374-One might consider protecting states based on their \emph{boundary outflux}
375-$\Phi_{\partial}(\boldsymbol{x},t):=p(\boldsymbol{x},t)\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}\in J'}\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{y}\boldsymbol{x}}$
376:---the rate at which probability leaves $\boldsymbol{x}$ to states outside the current active set---rather than total flux $\Phi(\boldsymbol{x},t)=p(\boldsymbol{x},t)w(\boldsymbol{x})$.
377-Total flux is preferable for two reasons.
378-First, genuine bottleneck states send their transitions primarily to high-probability metastable states already in $J$, so their boundary outflux is approximately zero; using boundary flux would \emph{fail} to protect them.
379-Total flux $p(s_b)w(s_b)$ remains large because $w(s_b)$ is large: it measures the rate at which probability \emph{cycles through} $s_b$ regardless of where those transitions lead, which is precisely the signature of a fast-transit connector.
380-Second, computing $\Phi_{\partial}$ requires knowing $J'$, which is determined by the pruning decision itself, creating a circular dependency. Total flux $p(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot(-\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{x}})$ is a byproduct of the generator diagonal, available without any knowledge of the pruning outcome.
381-\end{remark}

618-\begin{theorem}[Local model error bound]
619-\label{thm:local-model-error}
620-Over a single time step $[t_n, t_{n+1}]$, the local model error satisfies
621-\begin{equation}
622:\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})\|_1 \leq \int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} (\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(s) + \Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(s)) \; ds.
623-\end{equation}
624:where $\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{out}}$ are boundary fluxes entering and leaving the truncation boundary respectively (Eq.~\eqref{eq:flux-defs}).
625-\end{theorem}

702-\begin{corollary}[Combined flux and probability error control]
703-\label{cor:local-error-pruning}
704-Under flux-based pruning with probability tolerance $\alpha$ and flux tolerance $\epsilon_{\mathrm{flux}}$, the local error over $[t_n, t_{n+1}]$ satisfies, to leading order in $\Delta t_n$,
705-\begin{equation}
706:\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})\|_1 \lesssim (\epsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} \cdot \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(t_n) + \alpha \cdot w_{\max}) \cdot \Delta t_n,
707-\end{equation}
708-[Omitted long context line]
709-\end{corollary}
710-\begin{proof}
711-By Theorem~\ref{thm:local-model-error}, the local model error satisfies
712-\begin{equation}
713:\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})\|_1 \leq \int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} (\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(s) + \Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(s)) \, ds \approx (\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(t_n) + \Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(t_n)) \cdot \Delta t_n
714-\end{equation}
715:for small $\Delta t_n$. Since $J'_n$ carries negligible probability after pruning, $\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(t_n) \approx 0$ and the error is dominated by $\Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(t_n)$.
716-
717:By Algorithm~\ref{alg:flux-prune}, the pruned set $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{prune}} = \mathcal{C} \setminus \mathcal{P}$ consists of states with total probability mass at most $\alpha$ (from quantile selection) and flux below $\epsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} \cdot \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}$ (failed flux protection). The flux contribution from pruned states satisfies
718-\begin{equation}
719-\sum_{x \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{prune}}} p(x, t_n) \cdot w(x) \leq w_{\max} \sum_{x \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{prune}}} p(x, t_n) \leq \alpha \cdot w_{\max}.
720-\end{equation}
721:States retained by flux protection contribute at most $\epsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} \cdot \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(t_n)$ to boundary flux by construction. Combining these contributions yields $\Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(t_n) \leq \epsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} \cdot \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(t_n) + \alpha \cdot w_{\max}$, which gives the stated bound.
722-\end{proof}

726-\begin{corollary}[Adaptive time step]
727-\label{cor:flux-timestep}
728:Let $\kappa_n := \lambda_{\max}(J_n,t_n)/\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(J_n,t_n) \geq 1$ denote the \emph{spatial stiffness ratio}, where $\lambda_{\max}(J_n,t_n) := \max_{\boldsymbol{x}\in J_n} w(\boldsymbol{x})$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(J_n,t_n) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in J_n}p(\boldsymbol{x},t_n)\,w(\boldsymbol{x})$.
729:The \emph{total-flux rule}
730-\begin{equation}
731-\label{eq:dt-total}
732:\Delta t_n = \frac{\epsilon_{\Delta t}}{\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(J_n, t_n)}
733-\end{equation}
734-guarantees $\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})\|_1 \lesssim \epsilon_{\Delta t}$ to leading order in $\Delta t_n$, with expansion factor $\mu_n := \lambda_{\max}\,\Delta t_n = \kappa_n\,\epsilon_{\Delta t}$.
735-\end{corollary}
736-
737-\begin{proof}
738-By Theorem~\ref{thm:local-model-error}, approximating boundary fluxes as constant over $[t_n,t_{n+1}]$:
739-\begin{equation}
740:\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})\|_1 \approx [\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(t_n) + \Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(t_n)]\,\Delta t_n.
741-\end{equation}
742-For the outflow, since the rate of leaving any state $\boldsymbol{x} \in J$ into the complement $J'$ is at most $w(\boldsymbol{x})$, i.e.,\ $\sum_{\boldsymbol{y} \in J'} \mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{y}\boldsymbol{x}} \leq w(\boldsymbol{x})$:
743-\begin{equation}
744:\Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(t_n) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in J}\!\Big(\sum_{\boldsymbol{y} \in J'}\!\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{y}\boldsymbol{x}}\Big)p(\boldsymbol{x},t_n) \leq \sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in J}w(\boldsymbol{x})\,p(\boldsymbol{x},t_n) = \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(J,t_n).
745-\end{equation}
746:For the inflow, all states in $J'\setminus\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{prune}}$ carry $p(\boldsymbol{x},t_n)=0$ (never in the active set), and $\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{prune}}}p(\boldsymbol{x},t_n)\leq\alpha$ by the quantile selection of Algorithm~\ref{alg:flux-prune}, so $\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(t_n)\leq w_{\max}(J')\cdot\alpha$.  Since $\alpha$ is chosen small this is negligible, giving the key bound:
747-\begin{equation}
748-\label{eq:key-flux-bound}
749:\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})\|_1 \lesssim \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(J_n,t_n)\,\Delta t_n.
750-\end{equation}
751-
752:Substituting $\Delta t_n = \epsilon_{\Delta t}/\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}$ into~\eqref{eq:key-flux-bound} yields $\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})\|_1 \leq \epsilon_{\Delta t}$.  The expansion factor is $\mu_n = \lambda_{\max}\,\Delta t_n = \lambda_{\max}\,\epsilon_{\Delta t}/\Phi_{\mathrm{total}} = \kappa_n\,\epsilon_{\Delta t}$.
753-\end{proof}

755-\begin{corollary}[Expansion depth for boundary control]
756-\label{cor:expansion-depth}
757-Let $\lambda_{\max}(J_n, t_n) := \max_{\boldsymbol{x} \in J_n} w(\boldsymbol{x})$ denote the maximum total exit rate in the active set, and set $\mu_n := \lambda_{\max}(J_n,t_n)\,\Delta t_n$.
758-In a CTMC with all exit rates bounded above by $\lambda_{\max}$, the holding time at each state is at least $\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda_{\max})$, so the number of reactions along any trajectory in time $s$ is stochastically dominated by $\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda_{\max}\,s)$.
759-A state at graph distance $d$ from $J_n$ therefore requires at least $d$ such events to be reached.
760-After expanding $J_n$ by depth $d$, the expansion-induced boundary outflux satisfies
761-\begin{equation}
762-\label{eq:expansion-outflux-bound}
763:\int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} \Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(s)\, ds
764-\;\leq\;
765-\mu_n \cdot \mathrm{P}\!\bigl(\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu_n) \geq d\bigr).
766-\end{equation}
767-For $d=1$: $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu_n)\geq 1) = 1-e^{-\mu_n} \leq \mu_n$, so the bound reduces to $\mu_n^2$.
768:The condition $\mu_n \le d$ is equivalent to $\Delta t_n \le d/\lambda_{\max}$; for systems with $\kappa_n = \lambda_{\max}/\Phi_{\mathrm{total}} \le d/\varepsilon_{\Delta t}$, the flux step $\Delta t_n = \varepsilon_{\Delta t}/\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}$ already satisfies this, so depth $d$ is guaranteed without any additional cap. For slow-start systems where $\Phi_{\mathrm{total}} \to 0$, an explicit cap is required (Remark~\ref{rem:max-propensity-cap}).
769-\end{corollary}
770-
771-\begin{proof}
772-Let $N(s)$ denote the number of reactions in $[0,s]$. Since each holding time is $\geq\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda_{\max})$, $N(s)\preceq\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda_{\max}s)$ stochastically.
773-All mass starts in $J_n$; reaching $J'$ requires $N(s)\geq d$, so
774:$\Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(t_n+s)\leq\lambda_{\max}\,\mathrm{P}(N(s)\geq d)\leq\lambda_{\max}\,\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda_{\max}s)\geq d)$.
775-Integrating and applying monotonicity of the Poisson survivor in $s$ gives~\eqref{eq:expansion-outflux-bound}.
776-For $d=1$: $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu_n)\geq 1)=1-e^{-\mu_n}\leq\mu_n$.
777-\end{proof}

789-\begin{remark}[Consistent parameter selection via spatial stiffness]
790-\label{rem:consistent-params}
791:Corollaries~\ref{cor:local-error-pruning}, \ref{cor:flux-timestep}, and~\ref{cor:expansion-depth} and Remark~\ref{rem:expansion-strategy} provide a self-consistent framework for parameter selection, governed by the spatial stiffness ratio $\kappa=\lambda_{\max}/\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}$:
792-\begin{itemize}
793:  \item \textbf{Time step:} use the total-flux rule $\Delta t = \varepsilon_{\Delta t}/\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}$ (leading-order error $\lesssim\varepsilon_{\Delta t}$, $\mu=\kappa\,\varepsilon_{\Delta t}$); for highly stiff systems apply the max-propensity cap (Remark~\ref{rem:max-propensity-cap}) to enforce $\mu\leq 1$ unconditionally \hfill (Corollary~\ref{cor:flux-timestep}),
794-  \item \textbf{Expansion depth and strategy:} use $d=1$ (ensured by $\kappa\,\varepsilon_{\Delta t}\leq 1$ or the cap); choose SSA expansion when $\kappa\approx 1$ (one dominant channel) and stoichiometric expansion when $\kappa>1$ \hfill (Corollary~\ref{cor:expansion-depth}, Remark~\ref{rem:expansion-strategy}),
795-  \item \textbf{Flux tolerance:} $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} \ll \varepsilon_{\Delta t}$, e.g., $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} = \varepsilon_{\Delta t}/w_{\max}$ \hfill (Corollary~\ref{cor:local-error-pruning}).
796-\end{itemize}
797-\end{remark}

1089-\begin{remark}[Depth-aware cap for slow-start systems]
1090-\label{rem:max-propensity-cap}
1091:The condition $\mu_n \le d$ is automatically satisfied whenever $\kappa_n\,\varepsilon_{\Delta t} \le d$. For \emph{slow-start} systems where $\Phi_{\mathrm{total}} \to 0$ at $t=0$ (e.g.\ the Bottleneck system with $k_1 \ll k_2$), the flux step diverges and depth-$d$ containment must be enforced explicitly. The implemented time step is
1092-\begin{equation}
1093-\label{eq:dt-cap}
1094:\Delta t_n = \min\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{\Delta t}}{\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(J_n,t_n)},\;\frac{d}{\lambda_{\mathrm{downstream}}},\;t_f - t_n\right),
1095-\end{equation}
1096-where $\lambda_{\mathrm{downstream}}$ is the exit rate of the fast downstream states first reached by expansion. In the implementation, the user supplies $\Delta t_{\max} = 1/\lambda_{\mathrm{downstream}}$ and the code applies the cap $d\cdot\Delta t_{\max}$, so increasing expansion depth automatically relaxes the cap proportionally without requiring any parameter change.
1097-\end{remark}
