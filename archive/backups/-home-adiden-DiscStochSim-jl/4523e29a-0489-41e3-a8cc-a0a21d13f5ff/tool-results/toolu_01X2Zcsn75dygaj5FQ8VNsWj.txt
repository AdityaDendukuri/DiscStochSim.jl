46:\section{Introduction}
47:The Chemical Master Equation (CME) governs the evolution of probability distributions for well-mixed stochastic reaction networks and is the canonical description of intrinsic noise in biochemical systems \cite{Gillespie1977, Wilkinson2006-so}. 
48:Formally, the CME is an infinite system of linear ODEs posed on a countably infinite lattice of molecular population states. 
49:Because the reachable state space typically grows combinatorially with copy numbers, the CME cannot be solved directly without an adaptive or carefully structured truncation scheme.

55:\subsection{Related Work}
56:\label{sec:related-work}
57:We review prior work on adaptive FSP methods and state space truncation strategies for stochastic chemical kinetics. The original FSP method \cite{Munsky2006} introduced fixed truncation with rigorous error bounds based on the probability flux leaving the truncated domain. Munsky and Khammash \cite{MUNSKY2007818} extended this framework to time-stepping variants that repeatedly expand the state space along reachable directions, providing adaptive control of the truncation error over time.

71:\subsection{The Chemical Master Equation}
73:\begin{definition}[Chemical Master Equation (CME)]
74:The \textit{Chemical Master Equation (CME)} describes the time evolution of the probability distribution $p(\boldsymbol{x}, t)$ over the state $\boldsymbol{x}$. For a single state $\boldsymbol{x}$, the CME is
76:    \frac{d}{dt} p(\boldsymbol{x}, t) =
77:    \sum_{k=1}^{M} \big[\alpha_k(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{\nu}_k)\, p(\boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{\nu}_k, t)
78:    - \alpha_k(\boldsymbol{x})\, p(\boldsymbol{x}, t)\big],
80:where $\alpha_k(\boldsymbol{x})$ is the propensity for reaction $k$ at state $\boldsymbol{x}$ and $\boldsymbol{\nu}_k$ is its stoichiometric change vector.
82:For the entire state space $\mathbf{X}$, the CME can be written in matrix form as
84:    \frac{d}{dt}\, \boldsymbol{p}(\mathbf{X}, t) = \mathbf{A}\, \boldsymbol{p}(\mathbf{X}, t),
86:where $\boldsymbol{p}(\mathbf{X}, t)$ is the probability vector over $\mathbf{X}$ and the \textit{generator matrix} $\mathbf{A}$ has entries
89:    \begin{cases}
90:        -\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M} \alpha_k(\mathbf{X}_j), & \text{if } i=j, \\
91:         \alpha_k(\mathbf{X}_j) & \text{if }\, \mathbf{X}_i=\mathbf{X}_j+\boldsymbol{\nu}_k, \\
92:    \end{cases}
94:and $\mathbf{A}_{ij}=0$ when no reaction connects $\mathbf{X}_j$ to $\mathbf{X}_i$.
97:\begin{remark}[Generator matrix properties]
98:The generator $\mathbf{A}$ satisfies:  
101:    \item $\mathbf{A}_{ii}\le 0$ (diagonal nonpositive),
102:    \item $\sum_{i} \mathbf{A}_{ij}=0$ for all $j$ (each column sums to zero),
106:\subsection{Finite State Projection Method}
109:The \textit{Finite State Projection (FSP)} method approximates the CME by restricting dynamics to a finite subset $\mathbf{X}_J\subset\mathbf{X}$. If $\mathbf{A}_J$ is the sub-generator on $\mathbf{X}_J$ and $\boldsymbol{p}_J(0)$ is the initial distribution restricted to $\mathbf{X}_J$, then
111:    \boldsymbol{p}(\mathbf{X}_J, t) \approx e^{\mathbf{A}_J t}\, \boldsymbol{p}_J(0).
115:\begin{theorem}[Classical FSP bounds \cite{Munsky2006}]
116:\label{thm:classical_fsp}
117:Let $A$ be a CME generator (\,$A_{ij}\ge 0$ for $i\neq j$, $A_{ii}\le 0$, and $\mathbf{1}^\top A=0^\top$\,), and let $A_J$ be the principal submatrix on a finite set $J\subset \mathbf{X}$. Denote by $p_J(t)$ the full CME solution restricted to $J$ and by $e^{A_J t}p_J(0)$ the truncated solution that ignores transitions to $J'$.
119:If for some $t_f\ge 0$ and $\varepsilon>0$,
121:\mathbf{1}^\top e^{A_J t_f}\,p_J(0) \;\ge\; 1-\varepsilon,
122:\label{eq:fsp-mass-cond}
124:then the following elementwise bounds hold:
126:e^{A_J t_f}\,p_J(0) \;\le\; p_J(t_f) \;\le\; e^{A_J t_f}\,p_J(0) + \varepsilon\,\mathbf{1}.
127:\label{eq:fsp-elementwise}

140:[Omitted long matching line]
141:At each time step, the method initializes $\boldsymbol{p}_J^{(k)}(t_k)$ from the previous solution, expands $\mathbf{X}_J^{(k)}$ to include newly reachable states, evolves the distribution forward in time, and prunes states whose probabilities fall below a chosen tolerance.
145:\label{alg:fsp-naive}
147:\REQUIRE initial state $x_0$, time range $[t_0,t_f]$, propensities $\{\alpha_k(x)\}$, stoichiometries $\{\nu_k\}$, step $\Delta t$
148:\STATE $t \gets t_0$, \; $J \gets \{x_0\}$, \; $\boldsymbol{p} \gets [1]$
150:  \STATE \textbf{Expand:} add neighbors of $J$ reachable by one (or preset $r$) reactions
151:  \STATE \textbf{Assemble:} build $\mathbf{A}_{JJ}$ on $J$
152:  \STATE \textbf{Evolve:} $\boldsymbol{p} \gets \exp(\mathbf{A}_{JJ}\Delta t)\,\boldsymbol{p}$
153:  \STATE \textbf{Prune:} remove states with $p(x)$ below a heuristic threshold
154:  \STATE $t \gets \min\{t+\Delta t,\,t_f\}$
156:\STATE \textbf{return} $(J,\boldsymbol{p})$ at $t_f$
160:This \emph{sliding-window} approach provides a general template for adaptive FSP methods with dynamically evolving state spaces \cite{Dinh2016}. Many variants differ in how expansion and pruning are performed, but they all follow the same broad framework which is to enlarge the state space to capture new probability flow, evolve the distribution on the truncated generator, and prune regions that have become irrelevant. 
161:The remainder of this paper focuses on refining and analyzing the pruning and time step determination steps within this general adaptive FSP skeleton.

246:\section{Methodology: Flux-Based Adaptive FSP}
247:\label{sec:methodology}
249:This section introduces a state space adaptation approach that preserves network connectivity through flux-aware truncation. We first define and characterize probability flux as a connectivity measure, argue that naive probability-based pruning can destroy essential pathways, then construct an algorithm that maintains structural integrity of the state-space graph while controlling computational cost.
253:Let $w(\boldsymbol{x}) := \sum_{k=1}^M \alpha_k(\boldsymbol{x}) = -\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}}$ denote the total exit rate from state $\boldsymbol{x}$. The \textit{outgoing probability flux} from state $\boldsymbol{x}$ at time $t$ is
255:\Phi(\boldsymbol{x},t) := p(\boldsymbol{x},t) \cdot w(\boldsymbol{x}).
259:[Omitted long matching line]
261:\begin{definition}[Total system flux]
262:The total probability flux of the system at time $t$ over state set $J$ is
264:\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(J,t) := \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in J} \Phi(\boldsymbol{x},t) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in J} p(\boldsymbol{x},t) \cdot w(\boldsymbol{x}).
268:\begin{proposition}[Total flux properties]
270:For the full state space $\mathcal{X}$ and any time $t \geq 0$, the total system flux satisfies
272:\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(\mathcal{X},t) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{X}} p(\boldsymbol{x},t) \cdot w(\boldsymbol{x}) = -\boldsymbol{p}(t)^\top \mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{A}).
274:Since $\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}} p(\boldsymbol{x},t) = 1$ for all $t$, the total flux represents a weighted average of exit rates across the state space. For reaction networks with mass-action or similar kinetics where exit rates grow at most polynomially with state values, $\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(\mathcal{X},t)$ remains bounded on compact time intervals and tracks the instantaneous activity level of the system.
275:\end{proposition}
280:\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(\mathcal{X},t) &= \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{X}} p(\boldsymbol{x},t) \cdot (-\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}}) = -\boldsymbol{p}(t)^\top \mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{A}).
282:Since the diagonal entries $\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}} = -\sum_{k=1}^M \alpha_k(\boldsymbol{x})$ depend only on the reaction network structure and state $\boldsymbol{x}$, the total flux evolves with the probability distribution.

304:\subsubsection{Flux-Aware Truncation Algorithm}
306:We propose a two-stage pruning approach that combines probability-based candidate selection with flux-based protection. The algorithm identifies states for potential removal using a quantile threshold, then protects low-probability states that carry significant probability flow.
309:\caption{Flux-Preserving Pruning}
312:\REQUIRE Active set $\mathcal{S}$, probability vector $\boldsymbol{p}$, generator $\mathbf{A}$, quantile tolerance $\alpha \in (0,1)$, flux tolerance $\epsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} > 0$
313:\ENSURE Updated set $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{new}}$ and renormalized probability $\boldsymbol{p}_{\mathrm{new}}$
316:\STATE Sort states $\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{S}$ in ascending order of $p(\boldsymbol{x}, t)$
317:\STATE Define candidate set $\mathcal{C} \subseteq \mathcal{S}$ as the minimal set satisfying
319:\sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{C}} p(\boldsymbol{x}, t) \leq \alpha \quad \text{and} \quad p(\boldsymbol{x}, t) \leq p(\boldsymbol{y}, t) \; \forall \boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{C}, \boldsymbol{y} \in \mathcal{S} \setminus \mathcal{C}
321:\STATE \textit{Stage 2: Flux-Based Protection}
322:\FOR{each $\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{S}$}
323:    \STATE Compute exit rate: $w(\boldsymbol{x}) = -\mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}}$
324:    \STATE Compute flux: $\Phi(\boldsymbol{x}, t) = p(\boldsymbol{x}, t) \cdot w(\boldsymbol{x})$
326:\STATE Compute total flux: $\Phi_{\mathrm{total}} = \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{S}} \Phi(\boldsymbol{x}, t)$
327:\STATE Set flux threshold: $\Phi_{\mathrm{thr}} = \epsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} \cdot \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}$
328:\STATE Define protected set: $\mathcal{P} = \{\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathcal{C} : \Phi(\boldsymbol{x}, t) \geq \Phi_{\mathrm{thr}}\}$
329:\STATE Define pruning set: $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{prune}} = \mathcal{C} \setminus \mathcal{P}$
332:\STATE $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{new}} \gets \mathcal{S} \setminus \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{prune}}$
333:\STATE $\boldsymbol{p}_{\mathrm{new}} \gets \boldsymbol{p}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{new}}} / \|\boldsymbol{p}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{new}}}\|_1$ \quad (restrict and renormalize)

504:\begin{proposition}[Compressed generator properties]
505:\label{prop:compressed-generator}
506:The compressed generator $\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ}$ satisfies:
508:\item $\mathbf{1}^\top \widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ} = \mathbf{0}^\top$ (zero column sums, hence mass conservation),
509:\item $\widetilde{A}_{\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{y}} \geq 0$ for $\boldsymbol{x} \neq \boldsymbol{y}$ (non-negative off-diagonals),
510:\item $\widetilde{A}_{\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{x}} \leq 0$ for all $\boldsymbol{x}$ (non-positive diagonal),
511:\item $e^{t\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ}} \geq 0$ and $\|e^{t\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ}}\|_{1 \to 1} = 1$ for all $t \geq 0$ (generates a positive stochastic semigroup).
513:\end{proposition}
516:Properties (1)-(3) follow directly from the construction of $\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ}$. For property (1), using Eq.~\eqref{eq:row-sum-identities} and $\mathbf{1}^\top \diag(\boldsymbol{v}) = \boldsymbol{v}^\top$,
520:[Omitted long matching line]
522:\frac{d}{dt}\|\boldsymbol{p}(t)\|_1 = \mathbf{1}^\top \frac{d\boldsymbol{p}}{dt} = \mathbf{1}^\top \widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ}\boldsymbol{p}(t) = (\mathbf{1}^\top \widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ})\boldsymbol{p}(t) = 0,
524:by property (1). Therefore, $\|\boldsymbol{p}(t)\|_1 = \|\boldsymbol{p}(0)\|_1 = 1$ for all $t \geq 0$. Taking the supremum over all initial conditions yields $\|e^{t\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ}}\|_{1 \to 1} = 1$. Next, we prove positivity: by properties (2)-(3), choose $\alpha \geq \max_{\boldsymbol{x}} |\widetilde{A}_{\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{x}}|$ large enough that $\mathbf{B} := \widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ} + \alpha I$ has all non-negative entries. Then
531:Since $\mathbf{B} \geq 0$, each factor $I + \frac{t}{n}\mathbf{B} \geq 0$ for $t \geq 0$ and $n$ sufficiently large. Non-negative matrices are closed under multiplication, so $(I + \frac{t}{n}\mathbf{B})^n \geq 0$ for all $n$. Taking the limit and multiplying by the positive scalar $e^{-\alpha t} > 0$, we can conclude that $e^{t\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ}} \geq 0$
533:We now establish the contraction property for the truncated generator $\mathbf{A}_{JJ}$, which is needed for the local and global error bounds.
537:The truncated generator $\mathbf{A}_{JJ}$ with non-positive column sums generates a positive contraction semigroup satisfying $e^{t\mathbf{A}_{JJ}} \geq 0$ and $\|e^{t\mathbf{A}_{JJ}}\|_{1 \to 1} \le 1$ for all $t \ge 0$.
541:For the norm bound, consider any $\boldsymbol{p}(0) \geq \mathbf{0}$ with $\|\boldsymbol{p}(0)\|_1 = 1$. Since $\mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{A}_{JJ} \leq \mathbf{0}^\top$, we have
543:\frac{d}{dt}\|\boldsymbol{p}(t)\|_1 = \mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{A}_{JJ}\boldsymbol{p}(t) = (\mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{A}_{JJ})\boldsymbol{p}(t) \leq 0,
545:which gives $\|e^{t\mathbf{A}_{JJ}}\|_{1 \to 1} \leq 1$. Positivity follows by the same shifting argument as in Proposition~\ref{prop:compressed-generator}.
548:\begin{remark}[Infinite-dimensional extension]
549:\label{rem:infinite-dimensional}

555:\subsection{Local Error}
557:We decompose the local error into model error from state space truncation and time-stepping error from numerical approximation.
559:\subsubsection{Model Error from State Space Truncation}
561:Consider the dynamics on $J$ starting from the true state $\boldsymbol{p}_J(t_n)$. The true restricted dynamics satisfy
564:\label{eq:true-restricted}
565:\frac{d}{dt} \boldsymbol{p}_J(t) = \mathbf{A}_{JJ}\boldsymbol{p}_J(t) + \mathbf{A}_{JJ'}\boldsymbol{p}_{J'}(t),
567:while the compressed dynamics evolve according to
569:\label{eq:compressed-dynamics-exact}
570:\frac{d}{dt} \widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_J(t) = \widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ}\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_J(t).
572:Define the local model error $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t) := \boldsymbol{p}_J(t) - \widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_J(t)$ with $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_n) = \mathbf{0}$.
576:Over a single time step $[t_n, t_{n+1}]$, the local model error satisfies
578:\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})\|_1 \leq \int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} (\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(s) + \Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(s)) \; ds.
580:where $\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{out}}$ are boundary fluxes entering and leaving the truncation boundary respectively (Eq.~\eqref{eq:flux-defs}).
584:Subtracting Eq.~\eqref{eq:compressed-dynamics-exact} from Eq.~\eqref{eq:true-restricted} gives the following inhomogeneous equation,
586:\frac{d}{dt}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t) &= \mathbf{A}_{JJ}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t) + \underbrace{\mathbf{A}_{JJ'}\boldsymbol{p}_{J'}(t) - \diag(\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{A}_{J'J})\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_J(t)}_{=: \boldsymbol{f}(t)}.
588:The solution of the inhomogeneous ODE is given by,
590:\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1}) &= \int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} e^{(t_{n+1}-s)\mathbf{A}_{JJ}}\boldsymbol{f}(s) \, ds + e^{A_{JJ}}\varepsilon(t_n). \\
592:We set the initial condition $\varepsilon(t_n)$ to zero as we start both systems with same initial condition. Eliminating the initial condition and taking the $\ell_1$ norm both sides, we get:
594:\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})\|_1 &\leq \int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} \underbrace{\|e^{(t_{n+1}-s)\mathbf{A}_{JJ}}\|_{1}}_{\leq 1 (\text{Corollary \ref{cor:truncated-contraction}})}\|\boldsymbol{f}(s)\|_1 \, ds \\
595:&\leq \int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} \left(\|\mathbf{A}_{JJ'}\boldsymbol{p}_{J'}(s)\|_1  +\|\diag(\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{A}_{J'J})\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_J(s)\|_1 \right)  \; ds \\
596:&= \int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} ( (\mathbf{1}^\top \mathbf{A}_{JJ'})\boldsymbol{p}_{J'}(s) + (\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{A}_{J'J})\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_J(s)) \;ds \\
597:&= \int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} (\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(s) + \Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(s)) \; ds.
602:While potentially conservative, this characterization provides a computationally tractable error estimate based on readily available flux quantities. We next analyze the accumulation of these local errors over multiple time steps.
604:\subsubsection{Matrix Exponential Approximation Error}
605:\label{subsec:time_stepping_error_analysis}
607:In addition to state-space truncation, we approximate the matrix exponential $\exp(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ}\,\Delta t)$ numerically. 
609:\begin{proposition}[Local time-stepping error]
611:For a matrix exponential approximation with tolerance $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ODE}}$, the error in a single time step is bounded by
613:\|\exp(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ}\,\Delta t)\,\boldsymbol{v} - \mathrm{Approx}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{JJ}, \Delta t)\,\boldsymbol{v}\|_1 \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{ODE}}\,\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_1.
615:Modern Krylov subspace implementations such as EXPOKIT \cite{Sidje1998} provide adaptive mechanisms to control this error by adjusting the Krylov subspace dimension based on the specified tolerance.
616:\end{proposition}
618:\subsection{Global Error}
622:Let $e_n := \|\boldsymbol{p}(t_n) - \widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}}_n\|_1$ denote the global error, where $\widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}}_n$ is the numerical solution on $J$ extended with zeros. For $N$ time steps with $e_0 = 0$,
624:e_N \leq \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} (\varepsilon_n + \tau_n),
626:where $\varepsilon_n := \int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} (\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(s) + \Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(s)) \, ds$ is the local model error and $\tau_n := \|\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{n+1} - \widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}}_{n+1}\|_1$ is the time-stepping error.
630:We track three sources of discrepancy:
632:\item $\boldsymbol{p}(t_n)$: true solution on full state space
633:\item $\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_n$: exact solution of compressed dynamics on $J_n$ 
634:\item $\widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}}_n$: numerical approximation on $J_n$
639:e_n = \|\boldsymbol{p}(t_n) - \widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}}_n\|_1 \leq \|\boldsymbol{p}(t_n) - \widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_n\|_1 + \|\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_n - \widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}}_n\|_1.
642:For the first term (model error), Theorem~\ref{thm:local-model-error} and the contraction property give:
644:\|\boldsymbol{p}(t_{n+1}) - \widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{n+1}\|_1 
645:&\leq \|\boldsymbol{p}(t_n) - \widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_n\|_1 + \varepsilon_n.
648:For the second term (numerical error), Proposition~\ref{prop:local_time_error} gives:
650:\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{n+1} - \widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}}_{n+1}\|_1 \leq \|\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_n - \widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}}_n\|_1 + \tau_n.
653:Combining these yields $e_{n+1} \leq e_n + \varepsilon_n + \tau_n$, and recursively expanding with with $e_0 = 0$ gives the result.
656:Having established the local error bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:local-model-error}, we now connect the theoretical error quantities to the computational tolerances that control the adaptive algorithm. The following corollaries show how the user-specified tolerances: $\alpha$ (probability mass threshold for pruning): $\epsilon_{\mathrm{flux}}$ (flux preservation threshold), and $\epsilon_{\Delta t}$ (time step error tolerance), translate into error bounds.
660:Under flux-based pruning with probability tolerance $\alpha$ and flux tolerance $\epsilon_{\mathrm{flux}}$, the local error over $[t_n, t_{n+1}]$ satisfies
662:\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})\|_1 \leq (\epsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} \cdot \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(t_n) + \alpha \cdot w_{\max}) \cdot \Delta t_n,
664:[Omitted long matching line]
667:By Theorem~\ref{thm:local-model-error}, the local model error satisfies
669:\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})\|_1 \leq \int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} (\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(s) + \Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(s)) \, ds \approx (\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(t_n) + \Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(t_n)) \cdot \Delta t_n
671:for small $\Delta t_n$. Since $J'_n$ carries negligible probability after pruning, $\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(t_n) \approx 0$ and the error is dominated by $\Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(t_n)$.
673:By Algorithm~\ref{alg:flux-prune}, the pruned set $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{prune}} = \mathcal{C} \setminus \mathcal{P}$ consists of states with total probability mass at most $\alpha$ (from quantile selection) and flux below $\epsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} \cdot \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}$ (failed flux protection). The flux contribution from pruned states satisfies
675:\sum_{x \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{prune}}} p(x, t_n) \cdot w(x) \leq w_{\max} \sum_{x \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{prune}}} p(x, t_n) \leq \alpha \cdot w_{\max}.
677:States retained by flux protection contribute at most $\epsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} \cdot \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(t_n)$ to boundary flux by construction. Combining these contributions yields $\Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(t_n) \leq \epsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} \cdot \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(t_n) + \alpha \cdot w_{\max}$, which gives the stated bound.
680:[Omitted long matching line]
682:\begin{corollary}[Adaptive time step]
683:\label{cor:flux-timestep}
684:To ensure the local model error satisfies $\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})\|_1 \leq \epsilon_{\Delta t}$ for a given tolerance $\epsilon_{\Delta t} > 0$, choose
686:\Delta t_n = \frac{\epsilon_{\Delta t}}{\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(J_n, t_n)},
688:where $\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(J_n, t_n) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in J_n} p(\boldsymbol{x}, t_n) \cdot w(\boldsymbol{x})$ is the total system flux on the active set.
692:By Theorem~\ref{thm:local-model-error}, assuming fluxes remain approximately constant over $[t_n, t_{n+1}]$ gives
694:\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})\|_1 \approx [\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(t_n) + \Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(t_n)] \Delta t_n.
696:We bound each boundary flux separately. For the outflow, since the rate of leaving any state $\boldsymbol{x} \in J$ into the complement $J'$ is at most the total exit rate $w(\boldsymbol{x})$, i.e.,\ $\sum_{\boldsymbol{y} \in J'} \mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{y}\boldsymbol{x}} \leq w(\boldsymbol{x})$, we have
698:\Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(t_n) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in J} \Big(\sum_{\boldsymbol{y} \in J'} \mathbf{A}_{\boldsymbol{y}\boldsymbol{x}}\Big) p(\boldsymbol{x}, t_n) \leq \sum_{\boldsymbol{x} \in J} w(\boldsymbol{x})\, p(\boldsymbol{x}, t_n) = \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(J, t_n).
700:[Omitted long matching line]
704:Setting $\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(J, t_n) \cdot \Delta t_n = \epsilon_{\Delta t}$ and solving for $\Delta t_n$ yields the stated formula.
708:[Omitted long matching line]
711:\begin{corollary}[Expansion depth for boundary control]
712:\label{cor:expansion-depth}
713:Let $\lambda_{\max}(J_n, t_n) := \max_{\boldsymbol{x} \in J_n,\, k} \alpha_k(\boldsymbol{x})$ denote the maximum propensity in the active set at step $n$.
714:After expanding $J_n$ by graph depth $d$ (so that every state in the complement $J'$ requires at least $d+1$ consecutive reaction steps to reach from any state in $J_n$), the expansion-induced boundary outflux satisfies
716:\label{eq:expansion-outflux-bound}
717:\int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} \Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(s)\, ds
719:\lambda_{\max} \,\Delta t_n \cdot \mathrm{P}\!\bigl(\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda_{\max}\,\Delta t_n) \geq d\bigr).
721:To ensure this contribution to the local model error (Theorem~\ref{thm:local-model-error}) is at most $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{exp}} > 0$, choose
723:\label{eq:expansion-depth}
724:d_{\min} = \Bigl\lceil \mu + z_\varepsilon\,\sqrt{\mu} \Bigr\rceil,
727:where $z_\varepsilon = \Phi^{-1}(1 - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{exp}}/\mu)$ and $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF.
728:Substituting the adaptive step $\Delta t_n = \varepsilon_{\Delta t}/\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}$ gives the depth in terms of the \emph{spatial stiffness ratio} $\kappa_n := \lambda_{\max}(J_n,t_n)/\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(J_n,t_n) \geq 1$:
730:\label{eq:expansion-depth-stiffness}
731:d_{\min} = \Bigl\lceil \kappa_n\,\varepsilon_{\Delta t} + z_\varepsilon\,\sqrt{\kappa_n\,\varepsilon_{\Delta t}} \Bigr\rceil.
736:After expanding $J_n$ by depth $d$, every state in $J'$ lies at graph distance $\geq d+1$ from any state in $J_n$.
737:Since probability mass in $J'$ is zero at time $t_n$ (all mass resides in $J_n$), the outflux $\Phi_{\mathrm{out}}$ is driven entirely by mass that has diffused from the interior of $J_n$ to its boundary during $[t_n, t_n+s]$.
738:Because each reaction fires at rate $\leq \lambda_{\max}$, the number of reactions along any path in time $s$ is stochastically dominated by $\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda_{\max}\,s)$.
739:The probability mass reaching graph distance $d$ from $J_n$ (hence capable of flowing into $J'$) by time $t_n + s$ is therefore at most $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda_{\max}\,s) \geq d)$.
740:Hence $\Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(t_n+s) \leq \lambda_{\max} \cdot \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda_{\max}\,s) \geq d)$, and integrating using the monotonicity of the Poisson survivor in $s$:
742:\int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} \Phi_{\mathrm{out}}(s)\,ds
744:\lambda_{\max}\,\Delta t_n \cdot \mathrm{P}\!\bigl(\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda_{\max}\,\Delta t_n) \geq d\bigr).
747:$\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu) \geq d) \approx 1 - \Phi((d-\mu)/\sqrt{\mu})$,
748:requiring $\mu \cdot \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu) \geq d) \leq \varepsilon_{\mathrm{exp}}$ yields~\eqref{eq:expansion-depth}.
749:Substituting $\Delta t_n = \varepsilon_{\Delta t}/\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}$ gives $\mu = \kappa_n\,\varepsilon_{\Delta t}$ and hence~\eqref{eq:expansion-depth-stiffness}.
752:\begin{remark}[Consistent parameter selection via spatial stiffness]
753:\label{rem:consistent-params}
754:Corollaries~\ref{cor:local-error-pruning}, \ref{cor:flux-timestep}, and~\ref{cor:expansion-depth} together provide a self-consistent rule for all three user parameters, governed by the spatial stiffness ratio $\kappa = \lambda_{\max}/\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}$:
756:  \item \textbf{Time step:} $\Delta t = \varepsilon_{\Delta t}/\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}$ \hfill (Corollary~\ref{cor:flux-timestep}),
757:  \item \textbf{Expansion depth:} $d_{\min} = \lceil\kappa\,\varepsilon_{\Delta t}\rceil$ (leading order) \hfill (Corollary~\ref{cor:expansion-depth}),
758:  \item \textbf{Flux tolerance:} $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} \ll \varepsilon_{\Delta t}$, e.g., $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{flux}} = \varepsilon_{\Delta t}/w_{\max}$ \hfill (Corollary~\ref{cor:local-error-pruning}).
760:When $\kappa \approx 1$ (all reactions at comparable rates), a small fixed depth suffices.
761:When $\kappa \gg 1$ (spatially stiff: fast downstream reactions gated by a rare slow transition), $d_{\min}$ must grow as $\kappa\,\varepsilon_{\Delta t}$ unless $\varepsilon_{\Delta t}$ is reduced accordingly.
762:The latter trades temporal for spatial efficiency: taking smaller steps keeps each step's expansion cost manageable at the price of more steps overall.
763:In the bottleneck example of Section~\ref{sec:results}, $\kappa \approx k_2/k_1 = 10^5$ early in the simulation; the adaptive formula would require $d_{\min} \approx 10^5$, whereas fixing $\delta t$ such that $k_2\,\delta t \approx 1$ reduces $d_{\min}$ to $O(1)$.

1223:From an implementation perspective, several steps of the algorithm can be parallelization.
